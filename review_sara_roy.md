# Review: Spectacles: A Verified Compiler from Semiring-Weighted Automata to Zero-Knowledge Scoring Circuits

**Reviewer:** Sara Roy (Machine Learning and Formal Verification Integration)
**Score: 7/10**

---

Spectacles addresses a genuine and pressing problem in ML evaluation: the dual crises of contamination and score integrity that undermine trust in benchmark results. The framing is precise—existing solutions like VerifiableEvals (South et al. 2024) prove program execution correctness but not specification conformance, while contamination detection methods (Sainz et al. 2023, Shi et al. 2024) are statistical and post-hoc. The insight that these two problems can be jointly solved through a single algebraic abstraction (weighted finite automata over semirings) is the kind of conceptual unification that the ML-verification community needs. The value proposition for regulatory auditors is particularly compelling: reducing verification cost from ~€50K (re-evaluation) to ~€0.001 (STARK proof check in < 100 ms) would transform the economics of AI evaluation compliance under the EU AI Act. The seven covered metrics (exact match, token-level F1, regex match, pass@k, BLEU, ROUGE-N, ROUGE-L) represent the core of deterministic NLP evaluation, and the explicit acknowledgment that BERTScore, calibration error, and LLM-as-judge fall outside the WFA fragment demonstrates honest scoping.

From an ML methodology perspective, however, the system has fundamental coverage limitations that the proposal understates. The seven WFA-compatible metrics cover traditional NLP evaluation but miss the metrics that matter most for modern LLM evaluation. BERTScore, which requires neural inference, and LLM-as-judge evaluations (used in Chatbot Arena, MT-Bench, AlpacaEval) are increasingly the primary evaluation methodology for instruction-following models—the very models where contamination concerns are most acute. BLEU and ROUGE are widely regarded as inadequate proxies for generation quality (Reiter 2018), and their continued use in benchmarks is more legacy than best practice. A system that provides cryptographic guarantees for BLEU scores but cannot handle the metrics that practitioners actually trust creates an uncomfortable gap between the system's capabilities and the community's needs. The contamination detection via PSI is similarly limited: n-gram overlap is a crude proxy for memorization, and sophisticated contamination (paraphrased test items, concept-level leakage) would evade detection entirely. The proposal should more explicitly position itself as addressing a well-defined subset of the evaluation trust problem rather than implying comprehensive coverage.

The ML-verification integration is strongest in the differential testing methodology and weakest in the formal verification's connection to ML practice. The triple implementation of each metric (reference Python, WFA, circuit) with property-based testing via `proptest` is excellent engineering that bridges the ML implementor's world with the formal methods world. However, the 117K–142K total LoC estimate raises feasibility concerns from an ML research perspective—this is closer to a production system than a research prototype, and the ML community evaluates papers on conceptual contribution, not code volume. The Lean 4 formalization, while mathematically valuable, may be inaccessible to the ML audience at CAV's intersection with ML evaluation. The more pressing concern is the system's deployment model: who runs the STARK prover? If the model provider generates the proof (as implied by the threat model), they must run 117K+ LoC of Rust code correctly, have access to their WFA-encoded metrics, and generate proofs in ~47 minutes for 50 examples across 7 metrics. This creates a significant adoption barrier. The proposal would benefit from a clearer analysis of the sociotechnical dynamics: will benchmark maintainers adopt this? Will model providers voluntarily generate contamination certificates that might reveal borderline training data practices? The technical contribution is strong, but the gap between a working system and an adopted system deserves acknowledgment. The comparison with VerifiableEvals is effective and precisely targeted.
