# Review: Spectacles — A Verified Compiler from Semiring-Weighted Automata to Zero-Knowledge Scoring Circuits

**Reviewer:** Vasco Vasconcelos
**Persona:** Probabilistic Models and Formal Methods Expert
**Date:** 2026-03-02

---

## Summary

Spectacles presents a compilation pipeline from NLP evaluation metrics to STARK zero-knowledge proof circuits, mediated by weighted finite automata (WFA) over typed semirings. The key theoretical insight is that a substantial class of NLP metrics — those whose computation decomposes into recognizable formal power series over a semiring (Schützenberger, 1961) — can be compiled into deterministic WFA, which in turn admit a uniform algebraic encoding as AIR (Algebraic Intermediate Representation) constraints over the Goldilocks field F_p (p = 2^64 − 2^32 + 1). The system provides a Lean 4 formalization of the core algebraic structures (KleeneSemiring typeclass, Goldilocks field laws, semiring embeddings) and demonstrates practical STARK proof generation and verification.

As a researcher in formal methods with a background in type theory, process algebra, and the mathematical foundations of programming languages, I focus this review on the algebraic and type-theoretic aspects: the semiring theory, the KleeneSemiring typeclass design, the correctness of semiring embeddings into finite fields, the type-theoretic properties of the EvalSpec DSL, and the relationship to classical automata-theoretic results (Schützenberger's theorem on rational power series, Eilenberg's variety theory). The engineering is competent; my concerns are primarily with the mathematical foundations and whether the formal model faithfully captures the intended semantics.

## Strengths

**S1. Elegant KleeneSemiring typeclass hierarchy.** The Lean 4 formalization defines a clean typeclass hierarchy: `Semiring` → `CommSemiring` → `StarSemiring`, with separate structures for `SemiringHom` and `InjectiveSemiringHom`. This is a textbook-correct algebraic hierarchy that supports generic programming over semiring types. The decision to separate the Kleene star (`star_unfold_left`, `star_unfold_right`) from the base semiring axioms is sound, as not all semirings used in the system are star semirings (the counting semiring ℕ only has a partial star, defined only at 0).

The typeclass design also correctly captures the key theoretical observation: WFA evaluation is parametric in the semiring, so the same automaton structure can be instantiated with different semirings (Boolean for exact match, counting for BLEU/ROUGE, tropical for ROUGE-L/edit distance) and the compilation pipeline operates uniformly over the semiring parameter. This is the algebraic content of Schützenberger's theorem — that recognizable formal power series form a class closed under the rational operations — and the typeclass design reflects it faithfully.

**S2. Careful treatment of the tropical semiring.** The tropical semiring (ℕ ∪ {∞}, min, +) poses specific challenges because its additive operation (min) is not a group operation — there are no additive inverses. The Lean formalization handles this correctly through the `ExtNat` type (extended naturals with infinity), and the Kleene star discharge (sorry N5) correctly proves that `a* = 0` for `a ≥ 0` in the tropical semiring by showing that the n=0 term in the star series `min(0, a, 2a, ...)` is already the identity element.

More importantly, the two-tier compilation architecture correctly identifies that the tropical semiring's `min` operation cannot be expressed as a field operation in F_p. The Tier 2 compilation uses comparison gadgets with bit decomposition to compute `min` using arithmetic circuit techniques (subtraction + sign bit extraction), which is the standard approach in ZK circuit design. The formalization of gadget soundness and non-interference (`Circuit/Gadgets.lean`) provides the necessary algebraic guarantee that the gadget-assisted compilation preserves the tropical semiring semantics.

**S3. Goldilocks field formalization is mathematically sound.** The choice of the Goldilocks field F_p (p = 2^64 − 2^32 + 1) is well-motivated by its 32-adic structure (the multiplicative group has order p − 1 = 2^32 · (2^32 − 1), giving a 2^32-th root of unity for NTT-based polynomial arithmetic). The Lean formalization proves the field laws (commutativity, associativity, distributivity, identity, annihilation) by reduction to natural number arithmetic modulo p, using `Nat.mod_mod_of_dvd` and `Nat.mul_mod` for the key steps. The proofs are somewhat mechanical but entirely correct.

The NTT parameters (root of unity, Montgomery constants) are independently verified in both the Lean formalization and the Python test suite, providing cross-language confidence. The discovery and fix of the Montgomery inverse constant bug (`0xFFFFFFFF` → `0xFFFFFFFEFFFFFFFF`) demonstrates the value of formal specification: the correct value `−p^{−1} mod 2^64 = 2^64 − (1 + 2^32)` follows from the factorization `(1 − 2^32)(1 + 2^32) = 1 − 2^64 ≡ 1 mod 2^64`.

**S4. The embedding theorems capture the essential algebraic requirement.** The semiring embeddings ι: Bool → F_p and ι: ℕ → F_p are correctly formalized as injective semiring homomorphisms. The key theorems — `natEmbed_add`, `natEmbed_mul`, and the injectivity results — establish that the embedding preserves semiring operations for values within the domain bounds. The Boolean embedding caveat (∨ → + is only valid for deterministic WFA) is correctly identified and documented, with a reference to Proposition D.1 that deterministic WFA ensure at most one active path per step.

This is a subtle but critical point: the field embedding preserves the semiring structure only when the WFA execution doesn't exercise the idempotency of Boolean ∨ (true ∨ true = true ≠ 1 + 1 = 2 in F_p). The restriction to deterministic WFA is necessary and sufficient for correctness, and the formalization correctly captures this with the `h_det` hypothesis in `boolEmbed_add_det`.

**S5. The connection to rational power series theory is well-grounded.** The project correctly invokes Schützenberger's theorem (1961) — that recognizable formal power series over a semiring are exactly those computable by WFA — as the theoretical foundation for the 21/31 metric coverage claim. The coverage census is derived from algebraic properties: a metric is WFA-representable if and only if its computation can be expressed as a recognizable formal power series, which holds for finite-state string transductions with semiring-valued outputs. Metrics requiring neural evaluation, infinite-state computation, or context-free operations are correctly classified as non-WFA.

The Myhill-Nerode characterization in `WFA/Minimization.lean` (Nerode equivalence as suffix-indistinguishability) is the classical automata-theoretic foundation for minimization, and its use for WFA equivalence checking is appropriate. The extension from the Boolean case (classical Myhill-Nerode for languages) to the weighted case (Nerode equivalence for formal power series) is a known result in weighted automata theory (Berstel & Reutenauer, 2011).

## Weaknesses

**W1. The Goldilocks field embedding does not preserve tropical semiring semantics.** This is the most significant mathematical concern. The Tier 1 compilation uses a direct semiring homomorphism φ: S → F_p that preserves both operations. For Boolean (∧ → ×, ∨ → +) and counting (+ → +, × → ×) semirings, this works because the field operations coincide with the semiring operations under the embedding. But for the tropical semiring (min, +), there is no semiring homomorphism into F_p: the field has no operation corresponding to `min`.

The Tier 2 compilation addresses this by using comparison gadgets, but the mathematical question remains: what exactly is the formal relationship between the tropical WFA evaluation and the Tier 2 circuit? The Lean formalization has `tier2_soundness` proved by `sorry`, and the supporting gadget theorems (`comparison_gadget_soundness`, `comparison_gadget_completeness`) prove tautologies about `Nat.min` rather than properties of the circuit constraints. The gap between "the gadget computes min correctly" (which is proved, trivially) and "the circuit constraints encoding the gadget are satisfiable if and only if the output is the correct tropical WFA weight" (which is not proved) is precisely where the soundness of Tier 2 compilation must be established.

This gap matters because the tropical semiring is used for ROUGE-L and edit distance — metrics where the WFA evaluation involves shortest-path-style computation (Viterbi algorithm). If the comparison gadgets' bit decomposition introduces off-by-one errors or handles the infinity element incorrectly, the circuit could produce wrong results. The 62-bit decomposition for values in [0, 2^62) presumably suffices for practical sequence lengths, but the formal guarantee should cover this range restriction.

**W2. The EvalSpec DSL lacks a formal type system.** The project mentions an "EvalSpec DSL" for specifying metrics, with a parser, type checker, compiler, and semantics (`crates/spectacles-core/src/evalspec/`). The Lean correspondence document references a theorem `eval_equiv_wfa` stating that well-typed EvalSpec terms compile to semantically equivalent WFA. However, the Lean formalization does not contain an EvalSpec type system — there is no `EvalSpec.lean` file defining the syntax, typing rules, or denotational semantics.

This is a significant omission from a type-theoretic perspective. The EvalSpec DSL is the interface between the user's metric specification and the WFA compiler. If the DSL's type system is implemented only in Rust without a corresponding Lean specification, then there is no formal guarantee that the type checker correctly identifies well-typed terms, that the compiler preserves typing (type preservation), or that well-typed terms have the intended semantics (type soundness). The `eval_equiv_wfa` theorem, if it exists, would need to reference a formal definition of "well-typed EvalSpec term," which is absent from the Lean formalization.

The `EVALSPEC_FORMAL.md` documentation (which I note is 30+ KB and too large for easy inspection) presumably provides the formal grammar and typing rules, but without a corresponding Lean formalization, these are paper specifications, not machine-checked ones. The gap between a paper type system and a machine-checked type system is exactly the kind of gap that formal verification is supposed to close.

**W3. The semiring-parametric AIR encoding conflates algebraic and circuit-level concerns.** The claim of "semiring-parametric AIR trace encoding" suggests that the AIR constraint generation is generic over the semiring parameter, producing correct constraints for any semiring instance. But the two-tier compilation architecture contradicts this: Tier 1 uses direct embedding (works for some semirings), Tier 2 uses gadgets (required for others). The AIR encoding is not truly semiring-parametric — it's a case split on the semiring type, with different compilation strategies for different semirings.

From a formal methods perspective, the clean approach would be to define a single compilation function `compile : WFA S → AIR` that works for all semiring instances, with the semiring-specific behavior encapsulated in the semiring homomorphism. The current architecture requires the compiler to know which semiring type it's dealing with and select the appropriate strategy. This is reflected in the Lean formalization, which has separate theorems (`tier1_soundness`, `tier2_soundness`) for the two tiers. A unified compilation theorem would be preferable but may be difficult to achieve given the fundamental difference between embeddable and non-embeddable semirings.

The deeper issue is that the semiring typeclass hierarchy doesn't capture the algebraic property that distinguishes Tier 1 from Tier 2: the existence of an injective semiring homomorphism into F_p. This property could be captured as a typeclass constraint (e.g., `EmbeddableSemiring S` with a proof that an embedding exists), which would make the tier selection type-safe and verifiable. Currently, the tier selection appears to be a runtime decision in the Rust compiler.

**W4. The relationship to Schützenberger's theorem is invoked but not formalized.** The project cites Schützenberger's theorem as the foundation for the 21/31 coverage claim, but the theorem itself is not formalized in Lean. The Lean formalization proves semiring axioms, embedding properties, and circuit-level lemmas, but it does not prove that WFA evaluation computes a recognizable formal power series, or that the metric-to-WFA compilation preserves the metric's input-output behavior for all inputs.

This is understandable — formalizing Schützenberger's theorem in Lean 4 would be a substantial project in its own right, potentially requiring formalization of formal language theory, Kleene algebra, and the equivalence between recognizable and rational formal power series. But without this formalization, the claim that "21/31 metrics are WFA-representable" rests on informal mathematical reasoning, not on the machine-checked foundation that the project advertises. The Lean formalization proves properties of the circuit layer (Layer 2 of the trust model) and the algebraic layer (semiring axioms), but not the specification layer (Layer 1: metric → WFA correctness).

The differential testing (68,748 tests) partially compensates, but it tests specific metric implementations against specific WFA implementations, not the general theorem that a class of computations is WFA-representable.

**W5. The counting semiring's partial Kleene star is problematic for the typeclass design.** The `StarSemiring` typeclass requires a total `star` function satisfying the left and right unfold laws for all elements. But the counting semiring (ℕ, +, ×) has a Kleene star defined only at 0: for n > 0, the series 1 + n + n² + ... diverges. The Lean formalization handles this with `countingStar : Nat → Option Nat`, returning `some 1` for 0 and `none` otherwise.

This creates a design tension: the `StarSemiring` typeclass expects `star : S → S` (total), but the counting semiring's star is partial (`Nat → Option Nat`). If `Nat` is instantiated as a `StarSemiring`, the star must either be made total (e.g., by defining `star n = 0` for n > 0, violating the unfold laws) or the typeclass must be modified to support partiality (e.g., via `Option` or dependent types). The current formalization seems to avoid this issue by not instantiating `Nat` as a `StarSemiring`, but this means the Kleene star properties are not available for the counting semiring in the generic WFA evaluation pipeline.

In the context of NLP metrics, the counting semiring WFA (for BLEU, ROUGE-1/2, chrF) don't require the Kleene star — they are acyclic or have non-star cycles — so this issue may not arise in practice. But it's a theoretical gap in the typeclass design that should be acknowledged.

**W6. The Nerode equivalence definition uses `finFoldl` rather than standard mathematical notation.** The `NerodeEquiv` definition in `WFA/Minimization.lean` defines two states as equivalent if they produce the same "suffix weight" for all suffixes. The implementation uses `finFoldl` to compute the final weight as a dot product of state weights and final weights. However, `finFoldl` is a Lean-specific implementation detail, and the sorry N1a (partition base case) depends on expanding this `finFoldl` definition.

A more robust formalization would define the Nerode equivalence using the standard mathematical formulation (as a formal power series or as a suffix-indexed family of semiring elements) and then prove that the `finFoldl`-based computation matches this definition. This separation of specification from implementation would make the proofs more modular and less dependent on Lean-specific implementation choices.

Additionally, the sorry N1a ("requires expanding finFoldl definition and case analysis on state equality") suggests that the interaction between `finFoldl` and the `Fin n → S` function type is causing proof engineering difficulties. This is a common issue in Lean 4 formalizations involving bounded iteration over finite types, and the use of `Finset.sum` from Mathlib would likely simplify the proofs considerably.

## Minor Issues

**M1. The `SemiringHom` structure should include a proof that `f` is a function.** In Lean 4, all terms of function type are automatically total, so this is not an issue. However, the structure could benefit from an `DecidableEq` constraint on the codomain to support computational verification of the homomorphism laws.

**M2. The `WFA` type in `WFA/Basic.lean` uses `Fin n → Fin n → Fin m → S` for transitions.** This representation encodes the transition function as a total function, meaning every state-symbol pair has a defined transition to every state. For sparse WFA (most transitions have weight `zero`), this representation is inefficient. While this doesn't affect correctness, it may cause performance issues for large WFA and could be optimized with a sparse representation in future work.

**M3. The Goldilocks field is formalized as `Nat mod p`, not as the quotient type `ZMod p`.** Using `ZMod p` from Mathlib would provide access to the full field theory (existence of inverses, Frobenius endomorphism, etc.) and would simplify proofs that currently require manual modular arithmetic reasoning. The decision to avoid Mathlib dependency is understandable for build simplicity, but it results in more manual proof effort for basic field properties.

**M4. The `constraint_satisfied` definition is `True`, making the soundness theorems vacuous at the circuit level.** This was likely intended as a placeholder, but it means the Lean formalization does not contain a model of AIR constraint evaluation. The algebraic properties (semiring axioms, embedding injectivity) are proved, but the connection to the circuit execution model is entirely absent from the formalization.

**M5. No formalization of the FRI (Fast Reed-Solomon Interactive Oracle Proof) protocol.** The STARK soundness depends on FRI for proximity testing to the low-degree polynomial space. Formalizing FRI in Lean 4 would be a significant undertaking (it requires formalizing Reed-Solomon codes, algebraic geometry of evaluation domains, and probabilistic soundness bounds), but without it, the STARK soundness is entirely an assumption. This is standard practice in verified cryptographic systems — even CertiCrypt and EasyCrypt rarely formalize the underlying hardness assumptions — but it should be clearly distinguished from the algebraic properties that are proved.

## Questions for Authors

1. **What is the formal statement of the EvalSpec type soundness theorem?** If `eval_equiv_wfa` is claimed to be a Lean theorem, where is its statement? If it's a paper theorem, what prevents it from being formalized given the existing infrastructure?

2. **How does the Tier 2 compilation handle the infinity element of the tropical semiring?** The Goldilocks field has no element corresponding to `+∞`. Is infinity mapped to a sentinel value (e.g., `2^62 - 1`)? If so, how is the sentinel value distinguished from a legitimate weight that happens to equal `2^62 - 1`?

3. **Could the semiring typeclass hierarchy be extended with an `EmbeddableSemiring` class that captures the existence of an injective semiring homomorphism into a finite field?** This would make the tier selection type-safe and allow the unified compilation theorem to be conditional on the `EmbeddableSemiring` constraint.

4. **What is the relationship between the Lean `ExtNat` type and the Rust tropical semiring implementation?** The Lean formalization uses `ExtNat` (naturals extended with infinity); does the Rust implementation use `f64` with `f64::INFINITY`? If so, the floating-point representation introduces concerns about NaN propagation, comparison semantics, and precision loss that the `ExtNat` model avoids.

5. **Have the authors considered using Mathlib's `Finset.sum` instead of `finFoldl` for the WFA evaluation and Nerode equivalence definitions?** This would likely simplify several of the remaining sorrys (N1a, N1b) by providing access to Mathlib's extensive library of finset and sum lemmas.

6. **Is there a formal argument that the 62-bit decomposition in Tier 2 comparison gadgets is sufficient for all NLP metrics?** Specifically, what is the maximum weight that a tropical WFA can assign to a string of length L with W-valued transitions? If the bound exceeds 2^62, the gadget produces incorrect results.

## Recommendation

**Weak Accept.** The algebraic foundations are sound and the typeclass design is clean. The connection to Schützenberger's theorem provides principled theoretical grounding. However, the formalization has significant gaps: the EvalSpec DSL is unformalized, the circuit-level semantics are vacuous (`constraint_satisfied = True`), the tropical semiring embedding is handled by gadgets whose soundness proofs are tautological, and the key soundness theorems are sorry'd. The project demonstrates the feasibility of WFA-to-STARK compilation for NLP metrics but the formal guarantees are weaker than the presentation suggests. The mathematical content is sufficient for a tool paper; a verification paper would need to close the gaps identified above.

**Confidence:** 4/5
