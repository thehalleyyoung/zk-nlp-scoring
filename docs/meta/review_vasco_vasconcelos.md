# Spectacles: Machine-Checkable Proofs for NLP Benchmark Scoring

**Reviewer:** Vasco Vasconcelos (probabilistic_models_and_formal_methods_expert)

## Summary

Spectacles presents an elegant application of classical automata-theoretic and algebraic ideas to a modern problem: certifying NLP benchmark scores via zero-knowledge proofs. The core intellectual contribution is the observation that many NLP string-matching metrics admit representations as weighted finite automata over typed semirings, and that this structure can be exploited to produce STARK-verifiable arithmetic circuits. The connection to Schützenberger's rational power series provides a satisfying theoretical characterization of the method's scope: a metric is certifiable if and only if it is recognizable as a rational formal power series over an appropriate semiring.

From a type-theoretic and algebraic perspective, the typed semiring discipline is the linchpin of the entire pipeline. The choice of semiring determines the WFA's semantics—tropical for edit-distance-like metrics, probabilistic for likelihood-based scores, the Boolean semiring for exact-match predicates—and the typing discipline must ensure that semiring operations are preserved through WFA compilation and circuit generation. The paper reports 400-state WFA for BLEU-4 with competitive proof performance, suggesting that the compilation pipeline handles non-trivial automata. The 21/31 metric coverage figure, derived from the rational power series characterization, is a clean theoretical result.

The tension at the heart of this work is between the algebraic elegance of the formalization and the engineering realities of STARK circuit compilation. The former lives in a mathematically clean world of semiring homomorphisms and recognizable series; the latter involves finite-field arithmetic, constraint systems, and hash-based commitments. Bridging these two worlds faithfully is the project's central technical challenge, and the current solution—differential testing rather than verified compilation—leaves the bridge unverified.

## Strengths

1. **Sound algebraic foundations via Schützenberger's theorem.** The use of rational formal power series to characterize WFA-representable metrics is mathematically rigorous and provides a clean answer to the scope question. Rather than arguing metric-by-metric, the framework applies Schützenberger's characterization theorem: a formal power series is recognizable (i.e., WFA-computable) if and only if it is rational. This elevates the coverage analysis from engineering pragmatics to formal language theory, and the 21/31 classification follows from algebraic properties of the required semirings rather than implementation accidents.

2. **Typed semiring discipline as a unifying abstraction.** The decision to parameterize WFA over typed semirings is architecturally sound. It provides a single compilation pipeline that accommodates diverse metric semantics by varying the semiring. The tropical semiring for edit distances, the real semiring for BLEU-style n-gram precision, and the Boolean semiring for exact match all fit within the same algebraic framework. This typing discipline should, in principle, ensure that semiring laws (associativity, commutativity, distributivity) are preserved through compilation—though the verification of this preservation is incomplete.

3. **Elegant connection between algebraic semantics and circuit compilation.** The pipeline from WFA denotational semantics (rational power series) through automaton construction to STARK arithmetic circuits represents a principled compilation chain where each stage has a clear algebraic characterization. The WFA transition structure maps naturally to arithmetic circuit gates (semiring multiplication for transitions, semiring addition for path aggregation), and the STARK framework provides succinct verification. The reported 3,821ms prove / 1.5ms verify performance for 400-state BLEU-4 suggests the compilation produces reasonably efficient circuits.

4. **Honest coverage analysis grounded in formal language theory.** The admission that only 21/31 metrics are WFA-representable, with a principled explanation rooted in the limitations of rational power series (e.g., metrics requiring unbounded counting, context-free structure, or non-algebraic operations), is intellectually honest and theoretically informative. This is more valuable than inflated coverage claims—it tells the community exactly where the algebraic boundary lies and invites future work on extensions (e.g., weighted pushdown automata for context-free metrics, or algebraic extensions for metrics with bounded non-regularity).

## Weaknesses

1. **Semiring typing preservation through circuit compilation is unverified.** The typed semiring discipline is claimed to ensure semantic preservation through the pipeline, but this claim is validated only by differential testing, not by formal proof. The critical step—embedding semiring operations into finite-field arithmetic for STARK circuits—requires demonstrating that the finite-field encoding faithfully represents the semiring structure. For the tropical semiring (min-plus), this requires careful encoding of the min operation in field arithmetic; for real-valued semirings, it requires demonstrating that fixed-point or rational approximations preserve the semiring laws to sufficient precision. Neither of these is formally verified, and the 15 Lean `sorry`s may conceal precisely these difficult algebraic obligations.

2. **The denotational-to-operational semantics gap is not formally closed.** The Lean specification presumably defines the denotational semantics of each metric as a rational power series. The Rust WFA engine implements the operational semantics—the actual automaton execution. The correspondence between these two (the adequacy theorem, in programming language theory parlance) is the cornerstone of the pipeline's correctness, yet it is established only by testing. In type-theoretic terms, this is analogous to having a well-typed specification and a separate implementation with no typing derivation connecting them. The gap is not merely an engineering shortcoming—it is a foundational issue for the formal semantics story.

3. **O(n²) STARK scaling limits applicability to long-sequence metrics.** The reported O(n²) scaling of STARK proof generation with sequence length is a significant practical limitation. Many modern NLP tasks involve long sequences (document-level translation, summarization of lengthy texts), and quadratic scaling would make certification impractical for these use cases. The paper does not discuss whether this is inherent to the WFA-to-circuit compilation (e.g., arising from the product construction for multi-string metrics) or an artifact of the particular STARK backend, nor does it explore asymptotic improvements such as recursive STARKs or folding schemes that could reduce the cost.

4. **Limited treatment of semiring completeness and convergence.** For metrics that involve summations over exponentially many paths (e.g., soft alignment scores or expected values under alignment distributions), the WFA semantics requires the underlying semiring to be complete (i.e., to support well-defined infinite sums). The paper does not discuss whether the typed semiring framework includes completeness requirements, or how convergence is handled for metrics that involve potentially divergent series. This is a non-trivial concern for probabilistic semirings where normalization is required, and its omission suggests the algebraic framework may be less general than presented.

## Questions for Authors

- For the finite-field encoding of non-field semirings (tropical, probabilistic), can you characterize the class of semiring identities that are preserved? Specifically, is the encoding a semiring homomorphism into the STARK field, and if not, what algebraic properties are lost and what impact does this have on the correctness of the certified computation?

- The O(n²) STARK scaling—does this arise from the WFA product construction for metrics comparing two strings, from the STARK constraint system generation, or from both? Have you explored recursive STARK composition (e.g., STARK-of-STARKs) to amortize the cost, and would the algebraic structure of the WFA computation admit efficient recursive decomposition?

- For the 10 non-WFA-representable metrics, have you investigated whether any of them are recognizable by weighted pushdown automata or weighted tree automata, and whether the STARK certification framework could be extended to these more expressive models while preserving practical proof sizes?

## Overall Assessment

Spectacles represents a beautifully conceived application of classical automata theory and formal power series to a contemporary problem. The algebraic foundations are principled, the typed semiring abstraction is elegant, and the connection between rational power series and STARK-certifiable computation is intellectually compelling. However, the formal verification story falls short of the algebraic ambition: the semiring typing preservation is unverified, the denotational-to-operational correspondence is tested but not proven, and the scaling limitations are not fully analyzed. The project would benefit from closing the formal gaps—particularly the adequacy theorem between the Lean specification and the Rust engine—and from a more thorough treatment of semiring encoding in finite-field arithmetic. As a research contribution at the intersection of formal language theory and verifiable computation, it is novel and promising; as a verified system, it remains a work in progress.

**Score: 7/10**
