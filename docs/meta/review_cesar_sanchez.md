# Review: Spectacles — A Verified Compiler from Semiring-Weighted Automata to Zero-Knowledge Scoring Circuits

**Reviewer:** Cesar Sanchez
**Persona:** Formal Verification and AI Researcher
**Date:** 2026-03-02

---

## Summary

Spectacles proposes a pipeline that compiles NLP evaluation metrics into weighted finite automata (WFA) over typed semirings and then generates STARK zero-knowledge proofs certifying that benchmark scores were computed faithfully. The system claims a four-layer trust model spanning specification fidelity, Lean 4 formalization, differential testing, and cryptographic execution fidelity. The Lean formalization covers semiring axioms, Goldilocks field arithmetic, embedding injectivity, trace layout, and gadget soundness, with 12 of 17 original sorrys discharged. The implementation demonstrates 68,748 triple-agreement differential tests with zero disagreements across reference, WFA, and circuit evaluators.

The work is ambitious and addresses a genuine problem: the absence of tamper-evident guarantees in NLP benchmark reporting. The algebraic grounding in Schützenberger's rational power series theory is principled. However, as a formal verification researcher, I find the gap between the claims made and the formal guarantees actually delivered to be substantial. The formalization is incomplete in ways that undermine the central soundness theorems, and the reliance on differential testing to bridge the Lean-to-Rust gap is a significant methodological weakness that the paper underplays.

## Strengths

**S1. Principled algebraic foundation.** The decision to ground metric decomposition in Schützenberger's theorem on rational power series is mathematically sound. This provides a crisp characterization of which NLP metrics are WFA-representable — those whose computation can be expressed as a recognizable formal power series over a semiring. The 21/31 coverage figure follows from this characterization rather than from ad hoc engineering, which is a genuine contribution. The non-WFA metrics (BERTScore, COMET, METEOR) are correctly identified as requiring neural or infinite-state computation.

**S2. Honest sorry audit.** Unlike many formalization projects that sweep incomplete proofs under the rug, Spectacles provides a detailed sorry audit (`SORRY_AUDIT.md`) that categorizes each remaining sorry by criticality, estimated effort, and position on the critical path. The distinction between cryptographic assumptions (T1, T2 — inherently unprovable in Lean), Mathlib dependencies (N1b), and genuinely incomplete proofs (N2a, N3a) is valuable and shows intellectual honesty. The progression from 17 sorrys to 6 sorrys plus 2 axioms demonstrates genuine proof engineering effort.

**S3. Bug-finding validates the methodology.** The discovery of two real bugs — the Montgomery inverse constant (`0xFFFFFFFF` vs. `0xFFFFFFFEFFFFFFFF`) and the Lagrange interpolation constant-term corruption — through the formal verification process is perhaps the strongest empirical argument for the approach. These are subtle arithmetic errors that would be extremely difficult to catch through conventional testing alone, as they only manifest in specific numerical ranges. This vindicates the claim that formal specification has value even before full verification is complete.

**S4. Two-tier compilation architecture.** The separation between Tier 1 (algebraic, direct semiring homomorphism to the Goldilocks field) and Tier 2 (gadget-assisted, comparison circuits for tropical min-plus) is a clean architectural decision. It correctly identifies that the tropical semiring's `min` operation cannot be expressed as a field operation and requires bit decomposition gadgets, while Boolean and counting semirings admit direct embedding. The Lean formalization reflects this distinction through separate soundness theorems (`tier1_soundness` and `tier2_soundness`).

**S5. Comprehensive differential testing infrastructure.** The 68,748 triple-agreement tests spanning 7 metrics, multiple semiring types, edge cases (empty strings, single characters, Unicode), and adversarial inputs provide substantial empirical confidence. The fact that an independent Python implementation re-verifies the mathematical foundations outside the Rust ecosystem addresses the concern of systematic Rust-only bugs.

## Weaknesses

**W1. The two-tier soundness claim is unjustified by the formalization.** The central theorems — `tier1_soundness` and `tier2_soundness` in `Circuit/Compiler.lean` — are both proved by `sorry`. This is not a minor gap. These are the theorems that the entire system's correctness hinges on. While the paper argues that they depend on standard cryptographic assumptions (FRI + Fiat-Shamir), the reality is more nuanced: the sorrys absorb not only the cryptographic assumptions but also the composition of trace layout correctness (N2), degree bounds (N3), the transition constraint encoding faithfulness, and the embedding homomorphism preservation. The `h_stark_accepts : True` hypothesis is a placeholder that encodes nothing about what the STARK verifier actually checks. A critic could fairly say that the two-tier soundness theorems are stated but not proved, and the existing proofs of supporting lemmas (gadget non-interference, column disjointness, individual degree bounds) are necessary but insufficient to establish the main theorems.

Concretely, the gap between "all individual constraint types have degree ≤ 2" (proved) and "all constraints emitted by the compiler have degree ≤ 2" (`all_constraints_degree_bounded` — axiom) is precisely the kind of gap where bugs hide. The composition axiom requires enumerating every constraint generation path in the Rust compiler and verifying that no path produces a higher-degree constraint. Without this enumeration, the degree bound is an assumption about the implementation, not a theorem about the specification.

**W2. The Lean-to-Rust gap is a fundamental methodological weakness.** The trust model honestly acknowledges that there is no verified extraction from Lean to Rust. The Lean formalization is a specification; the Rust implementation is an independent codebase. The gap is bridged by 68,748 differential tests. But differential testing provides probabilistic assurance, not proof. The comparison to CompCert and CakeML in `TRUST_MODEL.md` correctly notes that Spectacles' approach is "weaker than CompCert/CakeML's closed verification," but this understates the severity: CompCert proves compiler correctness once and for all inputs; Spectacles tests correctness on a finite sample and hopes.

The risk is not hypothetical. The Boolean embedding caveat in `LEAN_RUST_CORRESPONDENCE.md` illustrates the problem: the embedding ι: {false, true} → {0, 1} ⊂ F_p preserves ∧ → multiplication, but ∨ → addition only when at most one input is true (deterministic WFA). This is a subtle semantic condition that differential tests might miss if the test suite doesn't include WFA configurations that violate determinism. The test suite would need to explicitly exercise this boundary, and there's no formal guarantee that it does.

**W3. The 3 sorry'd theorems on the critical path are more concerning than presented.** The sorry audit categorizes N2a (trace layout allocator disjointness) as "Medium" criticality with "~1 day" estimated effort. If it truly is one day of work, why hasn't it been done? The same question applies to N3a (tier 1 degree bound via foldl induction). These are not deep mathematical questions — they are proof engineering tasks within well-understood Lean 4 idioms. Their persistence after what appears to be significant proof engineering effort (12 sorrys discharged) raises the question of whether the remaining obstacles are actually harder than claimed.

More concerning is the `all_constraints_degree_bounded` axiom, which is estimated at "1–2 weeks" and is on the critical path for Theorem 6.1. This axiom is not a cryptographic assumption — it's a statement about the compiler's code generation. It should be provable by structural induction on the compiler's constraint generation, but the fact that it remains an axiom suggests that the Lean formalization of the compiler is insufficiently detailed to support such a proof. If the Lean model of the compiler doesn't faithfully reflect all code paths in the Rust implementation, the axiom could be true of the Lean model but false of the Rust code.

**W4. Differential testing (68K tests) does not substitute for formal proof.** The paper and documentation repeatedly cite the 68,748 triple-agreement tests as evidence of correctness. While impressive in scale, this number is misleading: the relevant question is not how many tests were run, but what fraction of the input space they cover. For a metric like BLEU-4 with a vocabulary of 50,000 tokens and sequences up to 1,000 tokens, the input space is approximately 50000^1000 — a number so large that 68,748 tests cover an infinitesimal fraction. Property-based testing (proptest) helps by exercising random inputs, but cannot achieve formal coverage guarantees.

The triple-agreement methodology is also structurally limited: it verifies that three implementations (reference, WFA, circuit) agree with each other, but if all three share a systematic error (e.g., a misunderstanding of the BLEU smoothing formula), all three will agree on the wrong answer. The independent Python verification partially addresses this, but only for the mathematical foundations (semiring axioms, field constants), not for the metric implementations themselves.

**W5. The coalgebraic bisimulation claim is understated and incompletely formalized.** The project claims to use "Hopcroft minimization + coalgebraic bisimulation" for WFA equivalence checking. However, the Lean formalization of minimization (`WFA/Minimization.lean`) defines Nerode equivalence and states the Hopcroft correctness theorem as an axiom (`hopcroft_minimizes_correct`). The coalgebraic perspective — viewing WFA as coalgebras for an appropriate functor and bisimulation as a coinductive relation — is not formalized. The term "coalgebraic bisimulation" in the project description appears to refer to the theoretical justification for minimization-based equivalence, but this justification is exactly what's missing from the formalization. Citing Lammich & Tuerk's 2012 Isabelle/HOL formalization is helpful but does not constitute a Lean 4 proof.

**W6. Trust assumptions in the cryptographic protocol are partially unaddressed.** The PSI security analysis (`PSI_SECURITY_ANALYSIS.md`) honestly acknowledges that the semi-honest assumption contradicts the adversarial threat model and proposes a commit-then-execute defense. However, the analysis identifies residual risks that are significant: (1) without a verifiable OPRF (VOPRF), a provider can selectively corrupt evaluations; (2) the post-protocol verification step requires revealing C_train, partially undermining PSI privacy; (3) no UC security analysis is provided. The commit-then-execute framework is a reasonable engineering mitigation, but claiming "contamination bounds via OPRF-based PSI" as a cryptographic guarantee (G2) oversells the current state. The guarantee is conditional on assumptions that may not hold in adversarial deployment.

## Minor Issues

**M1. Inconsistent sorry counts.** The README states "14/17 sorry-free theorems" and "12/17 sorrys discharged" in different places. The sorry audit lists 6 remaining sorrys and 2 axioms, which sums to 8, not 3 or 5. The discrepancy arises from counting methodology (original 17 sorrys vs. current state after adding new proofs), but the inconsistency is confusing. A single, canonical count should be used throughout.

**M2. The `constraint_satisfied` definition in `Compiler.lean` is `True`.** This is a simplified placeholder, but it means that the formal model of constraint satisfaction is vacuous. Any trace trivially satisfies any constraint under this definition. The soundness theorems built on top of this definition are therefore stating less than they appear to state, even if the sorrys were discharged.

**M3. The `h_stark_accepts : True` hypothesis.** Both soundness theorems take `h_stark_accepts : True` as a hypothesis, meaning "the STARK verifier accepted." But this encodes zero information about the verifier's behavior. A proper formalization would define a `StarkVerifier` structure with its acceptance predicate and show that acceptance implies trace satisfaction of all constraints. Without this, the Lean formalization is a mathematical specification of the algebraic properties but not of the STARK verification protocol.

**M4. Goldilocks field proofs use omega and native_decide.** The `fadd_assoc` proof uses `omega`, which is sound but operates on natural numbers, not field elements. The proof works because the Goldilocks field is modeled as `Nat mod p`, but this modeling choice means that Lean is proving properties of modular natural number arithmetic, not of an abstract field. If the Rust implementation uses a different representation (e.g., Montgomery form for multiplication), the Lean proofs don't directly apply to that representation. The differential tests again serve as the bridge, but this is precisely the gap that formal verification should close.

**M5. The `comparison_gadget_soundness` theorem proves `Nat.min a b = if a ≤ b then a else b`.** This is a tautology — it's the definition of `Nat.min`. The actual soundness claim should be that the circuit constraints (Boolean constraints + bit decomposition + carry chain) imply that the output column contains `Nat.min a b`. The current theorem doesn't reference the circuit at all. Similarly, `comparison_gadget_completeness` proves the existence of a natural number equal to `Nat.min a b`, which is trivially true. These are placeholder proofs, not real gadget soundness theorems.

## Questions for Authors

1. **Why are N2a and N3a still sorry'd if they are estimated at ~1 day each?** If the proof engineering is straightforward, their persistence after significant effort on the rest of the formalization suggests either that the estimates are optimistic or that the priority is misplaced.

2. **What is the formal statement of the two-tier soundness theorems intended to be?** The current `tier1_soundness` takes `h_stark_accepts : True` — a vacuous hypothesis. What would a non-vacuous statement look like? Would it require formalizing the FRI protocol and Fiat-Shamir transform in Lean?

3. **How does the Boolean embedding caveat interact with the differential tests?** Specifically, do the 68,748 tests include WFA configurations where multiple paths are simultaneously active (nondeterministic WFA), and if so, does the Boolean embedding produce correct results? The embedding is only valid for deterministic WFA, but is there a check that compiled WFAs are deterministic?

4. **What is the formal relationship between the Lean `WFA` type and the Rust `Wfa<S>` struct?** The Lean WFA uses `Fin n → Fin n → Fin m → S` for transitions; does the Rust implementation use the same index types and ranges? Are the bounds checked at the type level or at runtime?

5. **Can the authors provide a concrete example where the degree bound axiom (`all_constraints_degree_bounded`) would be violated?** This would clarify whether the axiom is truly a compositional statement or whether it's hiding a potential failure mode.

6. **The paper claims "two cryptographic guarantees: G1 (score integrity via STARK, 128-bit) + G2 (contamination via PSI)." Given that the PSI protocol lacks VOPRF and UC security, what exactly does G2 guarantee under a malicious provider model?**

## Recommendation

**Weak Accept.** The project tackles an important problem with a principled algebraic approach, and the bug-finding results validate the methodology. However, the central soundness theorems are unproved, the Lean-to-Rust gap is unbridged by formal methods, and the cryptographic guarantees are weaker than claimed. The formalization is a work-in-progress that demonstrates the viability of the approach but does not yet deliver on the promise of "machine-checkable proofs." I would recommend acceptance conditional on (a) discharging N2a and N3a (claimed to be straightforward), (b) replacing the vacuous `constraint_satisfied` and `h_stark_accepts` with meaningful definitions, and (c) revising the claims to more precisely distinguish what is proved from what is assumed.

**Confidence:** 4/5
