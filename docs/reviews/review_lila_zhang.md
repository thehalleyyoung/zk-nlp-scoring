# Review: Spectacles: A Verified Compiler from Semiring-Weighted Automata to Zero-Knowledge Scoring Circuits

**Reviewer:** Lila Zhang (Symbolic Reasoning and SAT/SMT Solving)
**Score: 8/10**

---

Spectacles makes a genuinely compelling contribution to the intersection of symbolic reasoning and verified compilation. The core insight—that NLP scoring functions decompose into weighted finite automata over semirings, enabling decidable equivalence checking and verified compilation to ZK circuits—is elegant and technically sound. The `KleeneSemiring` typeclass in Lean 4, parameterizing over star-semiring axioms with connections to Arden's lemma and Mathlib's `Computability.Language`, fills a recognized gap in the formal mathematics library. The verified WFA library using `Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) S` transitions with formal-power-series semantics via `Finsupp` is a lasting contribution that exists independently of the ZK application. The two proof-automation tactics (`kleene_dec` for equational Kleene-algebra goals via NFA equivalence and `wfa_equiv` for automaton equivalence via minimization and isomorphism) represent substantial tactic engineering. The decision to prove the algebraic compilation soundness theorem (`circuit_sound_algebraic`) generically over any embeddable semiring—instantiated per semiring via injective homomorphisms ι: S → 𝔽_p—is textbook-quality formal methods design: prove once, instantiate many times.

The Lean 4 formalization strategy raises several concerns from a proof mechanization perspective. The scoping to 12K–15K LoC (reduced from 25K) is pragmatic, but the sorry management discipline reveals that some of the most mathematically interesting components may remain unverified. Specifically, WFA determinization and Hopcroft minimization correctness are classified as "should-prove" rather than "must-prove," while transducer composition associativity and `wfa_equiv` completeness are "explicitly deferred." This is problematic because the equivalence decision procedure is one of the three headline contributions—its correctness proof should be must-prove. The pilot formalization of `circuit_sound_algebraic` for the Boolean semiring case (~800 LoC) is a wise de-risking strategy, but 800 LoC is modest for establishing feasibility of a proof that must generalize across semirings and handle the matrix-vector multiplication encoding of WFA transitions as degree-2 polynomial constraints. The tropical case (`circuit_sound_tropical`) requires genuinely new proof engineering for bit-decomposition comparison gadgets, and the proposal provides no estimate of its expected difficulty relative to the algebraic case. In my experience with mechanized proofs of arithmetic circuit correctness, the gadget-level reasoning (showing that bit-decomposition correctly implements min over a prime field) typically requires 3–5× the proof effort of the clean algebraic case, suggesting the tropical proof could consume 2,400–4,000 LoC of the 12K–15K budget.

The symbolic reasoning infrastructure for specification equivalence deserves careful analysis. The O(n log n) equivalence checking via Hopcroft minimization and coalgebraic bisimulation is well-established theory (Rutten and Silva), but the mechanization in Lean 4 must handle the weighted case where bisimulation involves semiring-valued rather than Boolean relations. This is non-trivial: the standard Hopcroft algorithm operates on DFAs, and its extension to WFAs requires the semiring to be commutative and the automata to be deterministic—conditions that the proposal assumes but whose formal verification adds complexity. The `wfa_equiv` tactic must construct either a bisimulation witness (for equivalence) or a distinguishing word (for non-equivalence), and the correctness of both directions must be proved. The evaluation plan correctly tests this against brute-force enumeration up to length 20, but the restriction to WFA pairs with ≤ 10 states may miss edge cases in larger automata where minimization interacts with the semiring structure in subtle ways. The broader question of whether the WFA fragment genuinely captures the intended semantics of all seven metrics is addressed by differential testing (100K random pairs), which is necessary but not sufficient—the gap between "agrees on random inputs" and "semantically equivalent" is exactly the gap that formal verification should close, yet the `eval_equiv_wfa` theorem (DSL-to-WFA semantic preservation) is must-prove while the WFA-to-circuit direction relies on the separately proved `circuit_sound_*` theorems. This layered approach is sound in principle but creates a verification pipeline whose end-to-end guarantee depends on the conjunction of multiple theorems, each of which must be individually correct.
